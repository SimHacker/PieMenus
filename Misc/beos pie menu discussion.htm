folta@cs.umd.edu (Wayne Folta) writes:
>
> I've seen circular ones. The idea, as explained to me, is that you can select
> any option with the same amount of movement -- just a few pixels in the right
> direction. (The menus look like a pie chart, with the options arranged around
> the slices.)

That's part of it.  Another part of the appeal is that most people, when
using a mouse, require visual feedback for precise control of distance, but
are able to control direction without visual feedback.  With pie menus,
you can often make your selection before the menu even has time to pop up,
if you're familiar with the application.  Selecting items from a piemenu
(even a cascaded one) becomes a gesture, which can become automatic.  But
unlike more conventional gestural interfaces, piemenus explain themselves
for the benefit of new or casual users.

Another part of it is that, even if you are actually watching the screen
and getting the visual feedback, the piemenu entries are easier to hit
accurately anyway, because their area expands as you move farther from
the center of the menu.  The farther you move the mouse, the less accurate
you have to be with your movement (and you still end up not moving very
far, in most cases).

> Linear pop-ups have favored options -- the ones that come up closest to the
> mouse -- while pie menus do not favor any options. OTOH, its not obvious to
> me how you can expand pie menus in the same way that you can keep adding items
> to a linear menu. Or what you do about long choice names.

Clearly, piemenus aren't for all situations.  Hopkins cites some research 
that indicates that piemenus are easiest to use when they have an even
number of entries, for reasons which aren't completely understood.  Also,
they get more difficult to use when they have more than eight entries.
They are most useful for commonly used menus which have a small, fixed
number of entries.  They are not suitable for dynamic menus (like lists
of open files, for example).  And choosing the choice names takes care.
But they are terrific in some situations.  If you've ever played the Unix
version of SimCity, you'll know what I'm talking about.

As for long choice names: it's better to avoid them, and since you only
want to use pies for a few carefully designed, static menus, you usually
can find good short names.  But the conventional layout algorithm can cope
with them pretty well, by doing it this way:


                A long choice name
                     \     /
                Quit  \   /  An absurdly long choice name
                       \ /
                    ---- ----
                       / \
                Save  /   \  This one's better
                     /     \
                   Pretty good

(apologies to those reading this in a proportional font).
